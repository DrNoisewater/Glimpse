Glimpse audio crackle — microphone AND system audio

This update builds on the prior Glimpse-FIFINE-AM8-Audio-Fix-Source release.
It targets the shared timing/buffering path, not a FIFINE AM8 hardware fault.

Confirmed source-level problem:
- In the previous version the same video/encoder thread called
  IAudioCaptureClient::GetBuffer for both sources.
- GPU frame conversion, MP4 H.264 WriteSample, and disk activity could delay
  both WASAPI endpoints. The 300-ms *output* staging buffer did not prevent
  WASAPI *input* capture-buffer overruns when that thread was blocked.

Changes:
- src/audio.cpp + audio.hpp: a dedicated COM-initialized worker thread for
  microphone capture and a separate one for system loopback capture. Audio
  capture continues while the video encoder is busy.
- Each endpoint drains its WASAPI capture buffer every few milliseconds,
  with a 500-ms requested device-side buffer (subject to Windows support).
- The existing timestamp-aligned stereo PCM ring is protected by a mutex
  and has five seconds of bounded headroom for occasional encoder stalls.
  AAC/MP4 writing stays entirely on the recording worker thread.
- Recording pause/resume uses a synchronized clock mapping with per-segment
  packet-aligner resets, and switches of audio mode join old workers safely.
- AAC input batches use 1024 PCM frames (an AAC-LC frame) instead of 480.
- Recording stop joins and drains both audio workers *before* the final AAC
  write and MP4 finalization.
- Existing gain controls, smooth peak limiting, and diagnostics are retained.
- No third-party tools, microphone drivers, or administrator permission needed.

Testing:
- Portable GCC/CMake/CTest: policy_tests and audio_tests pass.
- Windows WASAPI capture, video encoding, and final recording quality cannot
  be tested on this non-Windows runner. Build and test on Windows 10/11.

Build: Extract, open Glimpse, and double-click build-windows.cmd.
The window remains open, build.log is saved, and a successful build creates
portable\\Glimpse.exe.

Test: Record 10 seconds of mic-only speech and 10 seconds of system-only audio
while playing a video. If you still hear crackling, share each recording's
*.mp4.metrics.txt sidecar and (if possible) a short affected MP4. Those
metrics can help distinguish lost packets, bad timestamps, and overloaded
signals. OBS sounding clean makes a Glimpse software issue more plausible,
but this fix is not a proven cure until tested on your hardware.
